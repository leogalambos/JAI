# Boundaries and limitations

JAI structures specialist work, review, traceability and human decisions.
It does not become the legal, risk, architecture or business authority.

## Structured justification is not arbitrary truth

A complete dependency graph can still contain bad requirements, incorrect
observations or mistaken professional judgement. Appropriate sources and
substantive independent review remain necessary. Human acceptance of a defined
use does not turn an incorrect claim into a fact.

## Choose the assurance arrangement

Direct specialist work and ordinary workflows remain available alongside the
layered matrix. They do not silently create a full layered engagement.
Deployments requiring a particular entry route must enforce that choice at
their own intake boundary.

## Provider and host boundaries

Native provider behaviour, available tools and effective sandbox permissions
remain dependencies. Managed hooks and behavioural contracts complement those
controls; they are not a universal sandbox or enterprise identity service.
A context identity binds an admitted result to the issued packet, not to every
hidden implementation detail of a third-party model service.

Keep private signing keys outside agent access and protect public trust against
unauthorised replacement. The operator retains Git, publication, deployment and
reserved human authority. See [human authority](../operations/human-authority.md).

## Recovery and side effects

Content hashes identify material but cannot recover deleted files. Keep the
current journal, supporting material and session/enrollment files together.
Local append-only records are not independently rollback-resistant custody.

External APIs need their own concurrency, idempotency and reconciliation
arrangements; a local decision cannot make a remote system transactional.

## Publication and research notation

A projection can establish its relationship to approved source content without
proving that source content is correct. Rendering and visual quality are separate
from professional review.

The research description uses Prolog notation for selected symbolic decisions.
The current JAI runtime does not embed or execute Prolog. A six-by-six matrix
also does not itself confer formal SABSA certification.
