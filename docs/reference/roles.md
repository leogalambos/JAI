# Full role catalogue

## Analysis

- **Business Analyst** — Understand a business problem well enough that the right thing gets built, and specify it so it can be tested
- **Systems Analyst** — Bridge requirements and existing systems: what the systems do now, what they must do, and what has to change
- **Requirements Engineer** — Engineer the requirements set itself: elicitation, specification, validation and management over the full lifecycle
- **Process Analyst** — Model, measure and improve how work actually flows through the organisation
- **Enterprise Business Analyst** — Analyse problems that span domains, systems and business units, where no single team owns the answer
- **Data Analyst** — Answer business questions with data, honestly, including when the data cannot answer them
- **Business Intelligence Analyst** — Turn agreed metrics into reporting products people can trust and use without asking an analyst
- **FinOps Analyst** — Make cloud and technology spend visible, attributable and defensible
- **Product Analyst** — Measure how the product is actually used and whether changes to it worked
- **Service Level Analyst** — Measure and report whether services deliver what was promised, and make the gaps visible early
- **Configuration Analyst** — Keep the record of what exists, how it is connected and who owns it accurate enough to rely on during an incident
## Architecture

- **Enterprise Architect** — Keep the whole technology estate coherent and steerable against business strategy
- **Business Architect** — Model what the business does and needs to be able to do, independent of how systems currently support it
- **Solution Architect** — Design one solution end to end so that it meets its requirements, fits the estate and can actually be built and run
- **Application Architect** — Own the internal structure of one significant application so it stays changeable over years
- **Domain Architect** — Own the architecture of one business domain so that its systems, data and integrations stay coherent
- **Data Architect** — Design how data is structured, stored, moved and governed across the estate
- **Integration Architect** — Design how systems talk to each other so that coupling stays manageable as the estate changes
- **Cloud Architect** — Design cloud platforms that meet performance, cost, resilience and sovereignty requirements
- **Network Architect** — Design the network so that connectivity, segmentation and performance meet requirements at acceptable cost
- **Security Architect** — Design security into systems so that controls are structural rather than bolted on afterwards
- **AI and ML Architect** — Design systems that train, serve and govern models reliably and within their legal and ethical limits
- **Technical Design Authority Member** — Provide independent review of significant designs so that quality and coherence survive delivery pressure
- **Architecture Repository and Modelling Lead** — Keep architecture models, viewpoints and repository semantics consistent enough that published views are traceable, reusable and trustworthy across projects.
- **Identity and Access Management Architect** — Design identity, authentication, authorisation, privilege and federation as one coherent architecture across workforce, customer and workload identities.
- **Operational and Technology Resilience Architect** — Design end-to-end technology resilience across services, dependencies, failure domains and recovery capabilities so that business impact tolerances are technically credible.
- **Enterprise Applications Architect** — Own the target enterprise application landscape and the architecture decisions that govern strategic platforms, enterprise SaaS/ERP ecosystems, capability fit, integration boundaries and application rationalisation.
- **Secure Delivery / DevSecOps Architect** — Own the secure delivery architecture that turns security policy into enforceable Secure SDLC, CI/CD, software supply-chain, policy-as-code and developer control patterns without collapsing assurance into the delivery team.
- **Application Portfolio Manager** — Own application portfolio lifecycle decisions and evidence, maintaining a defensible view of business fit, technical health, cost, risk, redundancy and transition so invest/tolerate/migrate/retire choices are governed rather than anecdotal.
## Commercial, Sourcing and Enablement

- **IT Procurement Specialist** — Buy technology on terms that serve the organisation, with the right people involved before signature
- **Vendor Manager** — Manage suppliers after signature: performance, relationship, risk and value
- **Contract Manager** — Make sure what was agreed is written down correctly and then actually happens
- **Software Licence Compliance Specialist** — Understand licensing rules well enough to keep the organisation compliant and out of audit exposure
- **Pre-Sales Solution Consultant** — Show prospects credibly how the product solves their problem, without promising what cannot be built
- **Sales Engineer** — Provide the technical substance behind the sale: validation, integration answers and honest limitations
- **Customer Success Engineer** — Make sure customers get the value they bought, and surface problems before they become churn
- **Implementation Consultant** — Get the product working in the customer's environment, configured for how they actually operate
- **Technical Account Manager** — Be the named technical owner of a major customer relationship across support, adoption and escalation
- **Technical Writer** — Produce documentation people can actually use at the moment they need it
- **Developer Advocate** — Help external developers succeed with the product, and bring their reality back to the product team
- **IT Trainer** — Build the capability people need to use technology properly, measured by what changes afterwards
- **Technical Recruiter** — Find and attract people who can actually do the work, without wasting anyone's time
- **Resource Manager** — Match people to work across the portfolio, honestly, including when there are not enough of them
- **IT Financial Management / TBM Manager** — Own the transparent service/project cost model, TCO and technology-business allocation model that lets leaders make comparable investment, sourcing, optimisation and lifecycle decisions across cloud and non-cloud spend.
- **Bid / Proposal Manager** — Own the supplier's evidence-based bid and proposal process from qualification through submission, clarification and controlled handover, so the offer is complete, responsive, deliverable and authorised.
- **Commercial Pricing / Deal Finance Specialist** — Develop defensible supplier selling-price and deal-finance recommendations that connect delivery cost, cash flow, commercial terms and uncertainty to authorised return and risk appetite over the full contract lifecycle.
- **Estimation / Cost Engineering Specialist** — Produce and maintain a reproducible basis of estimate for supplier work and lifecycle cost, with calibrated methods, explicit uncertainty and a learning loop from actual delivery rather than unsupported numerical precision.
- **Independent Commercial Assurance Reviewer** — Independently challenge the evidence, completeness and consistency of supplier proposals, estimates, pricing and lifecycle commercial controls, reporting located findings without authoring the model or accepting management's risk.
- **Supplier Commercial / Deal Manager** — Own supplier-side commercial coherence across qualification, negotiation, contract handover, delivery change, service operation, renewal and exit, ensuring agreed commitments and economic risk remain aligned throughout the deal.

## Data, Analytics and AI

- **Data Engineer** — Move data reliably from where it is produced to where it can be used, at known quality and cost
- **Analytics Engineer** — Turn raw data into well-modelled, tested, documented datasets that analysts can trust
- **Database Administrator** — Keep databases available, performant, recoverable and secure
- **Database Reliability Engineer** — Apply engineering practice to data stores: automation, service objectives and safe schema evolution
- **Data Modeller** — Define what the data means and how it is structured, so that systems and people agree
- **Data Steward** — Take day-to-day responsibility for the meaning, quality and correct use of data in one domain
- **Data Owner** — Be accountable for a data asset: its quality standard, who may use it and on what basis
- **Data Custodian** — Hold and protect data technically on the owner's behalf: storage, access enforcement, retention and disposal
- **Data Governance Lead** — Make data governance operate in practice: roles, policy, quality standards and the forum that resolves disputes
- **Data Quality Analyst** — Measure whether data is fit for its purpose, and make the cost of poor quality visible
- **Master Data Management Specialist** — Establish and maintain single, trusted versions of core business entities across systems
- **Data Scientist** — Answer hard questions with statistical and machine learning methods, and say honestly how much to trust the answer
- **Research Scientist** — Advance the state of what the organisation can do, and publish results that survive scrutiny
- **Machine Learning Engineer** — Take models from notebook to production and keep them working under real conditions
- **MLOps Engineer** — Run the machine learning platform and lifecycle: deployment, monitoring, retraining and rollback
- **LLMOps and AI Platform Engineer** — Run the platform for large model workloads: serving, cost, evaluation, safety controls and observability
- **AI Solutions Engineer** — Design and build applications on top of foundation models so they behave reliably in a real workflow
- **AI Safety and Model Red Team Specialist** — Probe models and AI systems for harmful, unsafe or manipulable behaviour before users find it
- **AI Governance Officer** — Hold AI systems inside their legal, ethical and risk boundaries throughout their lifecycle
- **Data Privacy Engineer** — Build privacy into data systems technically, rather than documenting it after the fact
- **Metadata Manager** — Make it possible to find data, understand it and trace where it came from
## Design and User Experience

- **UX Designer** — Design how the product works for the people using it, based on evidence rather than preference
- **UI Designer** — Design the visual layer so that it communicates clearly, works at every size and meets accessibility requirements
- **Product Designer** — Own the whole design of a product area: research, interaction, visual and the outcome it produces
- **UX Researcher** — Find out what users actually do and need, using methods that produce trustworthy answers
- **Usability Tester** — Run structured usability sessions that show whether people can complete real tasks
- **Interaction Designer** — Design the detailed behaviour of the interface: states, transitions, feedback and micro-interactions
- **Service Designer** — Design the whole service across channels, including the parts users never see
- **Information Architect** — Organise content and functionality so people can find things and understand where they are
- **Design System Engineer** — Build and maintain the shared component library so that consistency and accessibility are the default
- **Accessibility Specialist** — Make sure the organisation's digital services are usable by disabled people, and can prove it
- **Content Designer** — Write the words in the product so that people understand what to do and what just happened
## Executive and Leadership

- **Chief Information Officer** — Own the enterprise IT capability, its budget, its risk posture and its ability to serve the business
- **Chief Technology Officer** — Set the technology direction for the products the organisation sells and own the engineering capability that builds them
- **Chief Information Security Officer** — Own the information security programme, articulate security risk in business terms and hold the organisation to its stated risk appetite
- **Chief Data Officer** — Own data as an enterprise asset: its quality, its governance, its lawful use and the value extracted from it
- **Chief Digital Officer** — Lead the change of business models, channels and ways of working that digital capability makes possible
- **Chief Product Officer** — Own the product portfolio, its strategy, its profitability and the discipline of deciding what not to build
- **Chief AI Officer** — Direct the adoption of AI across the organisation and hold it inside legal, ethical and risk boundaries
- **IT Chief of Staff** — Make the technology leadership team's decisions, cadence and commitments actually operate
- **VP of Engineering** — Own the engineering organisation's ability to deliver: its structure, its standards, its throughput and its people
- **Head of Platform** — Run the internal platform as a product so that stream-aligned teams can deliver safely without waiting on central approval
- **IT Service Owner** — Own one service end to end across its lifecycle: its value, its cost, its risk and its performance for users
## Governance, Risk, Compliance, Audit and Privacy

- **IT Governance Manager** — Make sure technology decisions are made by the right people, on evidence, within appetite
- **GRC Analyst** — Keep the governance, risk and compliance machinery running with accurate, current data
- **IT Risk Manager** — Make technology risk visible, comparable and owned, so the organisation can decide rather than hope
- **Information Security Officer (Local)** — Be the accountable security presence inside a business unit or country, close enough to the work to see what is really happening
- **Compliance Officer (Technology)** — Translate external obligations into internal requirements and prove they are met
- **Internal IT Auditor** — Give independent, evidence-based assurance about whether controls are designed and operating properly
- **External Auditor and Assessor** — Provide independent third-party assessment against a defined scheme, to that scheme's rules
- **Control Owner** — Be accountable for one control: that it is designed properly, operates as intended and can be evidenced
- **Control Tester** — Test whether controls actually operate, using a defensible method and sample
- **Data Protection Officer** — Independently oversee how personal data is handled and advise on compliance with data protection law
- **Privacy Analyst** — Do the detailed privacy work: assessments, records, rights requests and advice on new processing
- **Records Manager** — Decide how long information is kept, why, and make sure it is actually disposed of
- **Business Continuity Manager** — Make sure the organisation can keep operating, and recover, when something serious happens
- **Disaster Recovery Coordinator** — Make sure technical recovery actually works when it is needed, by testing it before it is
- **Third-Party Risk Manager** — Understand and control the risk that comes with suppliers, especially the ones you cannot live without
- **Policy Author** — Write policy and standards that people can follow, that mean something, and that can be audited
- **Crisis Manager** — Lead the organisation through events that exceed normal incident handling
- **Privacy Architect** — Translate privacy obligations and risk into solution and enterprise architecture constraints, patterns and traceable design decisions.
## Infrastructure, Cloud and Operations

- **Linux Systems Administrator** — Keep Linux systems running, patched, hardened and recoverable
- **Windows Systems Administrator** — Keep Windows Server and directory infrastructure available, secure and correctly configured
- **Cloud Engineer** — Build and maintain cloud infrastructure as code, within the agreed guardrails
- **Cloud Operations Engineer** — Run the cloud estate day to day: health, cost, compliance and incident response
- **Platform Engineer** — Build the internal platform that lets product teams deploy and run services safely on their own
- **DevOps Engineer** — Remove the handoff between building and running by automating the path to production
- **Site Reliability Engineer** — Make services reliable enough for the business by engineering, not by heroics
- **Kubernetes Platform Engineer** — Run container orchestration as a reliable, secure, multi-tenant platform
- **Infrastructure as Code Engineer** — Define the whole estate as reviewed, versioned, reproducible code so that no environment depends on remembered manual steps
- **Container Platform Engineer** — Build and maintain the container image supply chain and stack definitions so that what runs in production is provably what was built
- **Environment Manager** — Own the lifecycle of non-production environments so teams can test realistically without waiting for each other
- **Virtualisation Engineer** — Run the virtualisation estate: capacity, resilience, patching and lifecycle
- **Storage Engineer** — Provide storage that performs, protects data and scales predictably
- **Backup and Recovery Engineer** — Make sure the organisation can actually get its data back, under the conditions it will really face
- **Datacentre Technician** — Keep the physical estate working: installation, replacement, cabling, power and physical access
- **Capacity Manager** — Make sure there is enough capacity to meet demand, and not much more, at acceptable cost
- **Availability Manager** — Ensure services meet their availability commitments by design and by evidence
- **Observability Engineer** — Make the system's behaviour knowable: metrics, logs, traces and alerts that people trust
- **Middleware Administrator** — Run application servers, message brokers and integration middleware reliably
- **FinOps Engineer** — Engineer cloud cost down without reducing capability: architecture, automation and accountability
- **Sustainable IT Engineer** — Measure and reduce the environmental impact of the technology estate with defensible numbers
## Network and Telecommunications

- **Network Engineer** — Build, configure and maintain the network so traffic flows correctly, securely and fast enough
- **Network Administrator** — Keep the network estate documented, patched and operating within standards day to day
- **Network Operations Centre Analyst** — Watch the network and services continuously, and start the response the moment something breaks
- **Wireless and RF Engineer** — Design and tune wireless coverage and capacity so it works where and when people need it
- **Unified Communications Engineer** — Run voice, video and messaging services so that critical communication works, including in emergencies
- **SD-WAN and Edge Engineer** — Connect distributed sites reliably and cost-effectively, with policy applied at the edge
- **Firewall and Perimeter Engineer** — Implement and maintain network security policy enforcement without breaking the business or leaving holes
- **DNS DHCP and IPAM Specialist** — Run the naming and addressing services that everything else silently depends on
- **Network Field Technician** — Install, patch and physically maintain network infrastructure on site
## Offensive Security and Adversarial Testing

- **Penetration Tester (Network and Infrastructure)** — Find and demonstrate exploitable weaknesses in infrastructure under written authorisation, then explain how to fix them
- **Penetration Tester (Web Application)** — Test web applications against the full range of application-layer attacks, under authorisation
- **Penetration Tester (Mobile)** — Test mobile applications and their backends for weaknesses that reach data or accounts
- **Penetration Tester (API)** — Test interfaces for authorisation, validation and business logic failures that clients never see
- **Penetration Tester (Cloud)** — Test cloud environments and their identity and configuration weaknesses, within provider rules
- **Penetration Tester (Wireless)** — Test wireless networks for access control and isolation failures, without disrupting legitimate users
- **Penetration Tester (Thick Client)** — Test installed desktop applications and their local trust assumptions
- **Red Team Operator** — Emulate a real adversary against the whole organisation to test detection and response, not to list vulnerabilities
- **Red Team Lead** — Run offensive engagements safely, legally and to a standard the client can act on
- **Exploit Developer** — Develop reliable proof-of-concept exploitation capability for authorised testing and defensive research
- **Vulnerability Researcher** — Find previously unknown weaknesses in software or hardware and get them fixed responsibly
- **Binary Reverse Engineer** — Understand undocumented software or hardware behaviour from its compiled form, lawfully
- **Social Engineering Specialist** — Test human-layer defences under authorisation, without harming the people being tested
- **Physical Intrusion Specialist** — Test physical security controls under authorisation, safely and with an unambiguous get-out-of-jail letter
- **Open Source Intelligence Investigator** — Build an accurate picture from publicly available information, lawfully and without touching targets
- **Adversary Emulation Engineer** — Automate the repeatable execution of adversary techniques so detection coverage can be measured continuously
- **Breach and Attack Simulation Engineer** — Run continuous, safe simulation of attack paths to validate that controls still work today
- **Bug Bounty Programme Manager** — Run an external researcher programme that produces real findings and treats researchers professionally
- **Bug Bounty Triage Analyst** — Validate and prioritise external submissions accurately and quickly
- **Vulnerability Disclosure Coordinator** — Receive, coordinate and resolve externally reported vulnerabilities in the organisation's own products
- **Hardware and IoT Security Researcher** — Assess the security of physical devices where the attacker has the hardware in their hands
## Product, Delivery and Agile

- **Product Manager** — Decide what gets built and why, based on evidence of customer need and business value
- **Product Owner** — Maximise the value of the work the development team does by owning and ordering the product backlog
- **Technical Product Manager** — Own products whose customers are engineers: APIs, platforms, SDKs and internal developer capabilities
- **Scrum Master** — Make the team's delivery system work: flow, impediments, and the health of the agreed way of working
- **Agile Coach** — Raise the delivery capability of multiple teams by changing how they work, not by doing their work
- **Delivery Manager** — Get a defined body of work delivered across teams, dependencies and suppliers
- **Release Train Engineer** — Run the programme-level cadence: planning, dependency resolution and flow across a group of teams
- **Epic Owner** — Shepherd one large investment from hypothesis through to measured outcome
- **SAFe System Architect** — Maintain the architectural runway so that teams can deliver upcoming features without stalling
- **Project Manager** — Deliver a defined scope to agreed time, cost and quality, and manage what changes
- **Programme Manager** — Deliver a coherent set of projects and change activity that together produce a business outcome
- **Portfolio Manager** — Decide which investments the organisation starts, continues and stops, against capacity and strategy
- **PMO Analyst** — Turn raw delivery data into a trustworthy picture of portfolio health
- **PMO Lead** — Own the delivery governance framework: standards, assurance, reporting and the capability of delivery roles
- **Engineering Manager** — Build and sustain a team that can deliver good software repeatedly, and grow the people in it
- **Technical Lead** — Set and hold the technical direction inside one team, and keep its code and design healthy enough to change
- **Transformation Manager** — Make the organisational change stick: roles, processes, behaviours and capability, not just systems
- **Organisational Change and Adoption Manager** — Make the people-side of technology change adoptable, measurable and owned so that delivered capability becomes changed behaviour and realised value.
- **Benefits Realisation Manager** — Turn investment claims into measurable benefits with owners, baselines, dependencies and post-implementation accountability.
- **Data Migration and Cutover Lead** — Plan and govern migration and cutover so that data, dependencies, business continuity and rollback are proven before irreversible production change.
- **Enterprise Release Manager** — Orchestrate enterprise releases across products, platforms, suppliers and business domains, owning the integrated release calendar, readiness model and delegated go/no-go decision without performing the technical deployment.
- **Demand & Intake Manager** — Own the controlled entry of demand into the IS/IT portfolio: qualification, duplication/capability fit, minimum evidence, sizing readiness, risk/value framing and transparent prioritisation handoff.
## Quality and Testing

- **Test Analyst** — Design and run tests that find the defects that matter before customers do
- **Test Automation Engineer** — Build automated checks that run fast, fail honestly, and are worth maintaining
- **Performance Test Engineer** — Prove how the system behaves under realistic and unrealistic load, before production does it for you

- **Chaos and Resilience Engineer** — Prove the system survives the failures it claims to survive, under controlled conditions
- **Codebase Reviewer** — Audit an existing codebase end to end and report, in priority order, what will actually hurt — without changing a line
- **Release Quality Gatekeeper** — Hold the line on release quality in regulated environments where evidence, not opinion, decides

- **Ethics and Responsible Technology Lead** — Make sure the organisation considers the harm its technology can do, before it does it

- **Simulation Engineer** — Build models that predict real-world behaviour well enough to make decisions with

## SABSA Security Architecture

- **Chief Security Architect (SABSA Practice Lead)** — Own the enterprise security architecture practice and keep every security decision traceable to a business driver
- **Business Security Architect (SABSA Contextual Layer)** — Establish what the business needs security to achieve, in the business's own language, before any technology is considered
- **Business Attribute Profiler** — Translate business drivers into measurable business attributes that security architecture can actually be designed and tested against
- **Conceptual Security Architect (SABSA Architect's View)** — Define the security strategy, domains, trust model and control objectives that satisfy the business attributes
- **Logical Security Designer (SABSA Designer's View)** — Design the logical security services, policies and information flows that deliver the control objectives
- **Physical Security Solution Builder (SABSA Builder's View)** — Turn logical security design into a concrete, buildable solution specification
- **Security Component Specialist (SABSA Tradesman's View)** — Select, configure and integrate the specific products, standards and tools that realise the design
- **Security Service Manager (SABSA Service Management View)** — Run the security architecture in operation, across every layer, so that designed capability stays real
- **Security Domain Authority** — Own the security policy, trust relationships and risk decisions within one defined security domain
- **Security Risk and Opportunity Architect** — Apply SABSA risk practice so that security addresses both threats to avoid and opportunities to enable
- **Security Assurance and Measurement Architect** — Design how the organisation proves its security architecture is working, using measures derived from business attributes
- **SABSA and TOGAF Integration Architect** — Make security architecture and enterprise architecture one coherent practice rather than two competing ones

- **Security Analyst Tier 2** — Investigate escalated alerts to a defensible conclusion and contain what is confirmed

- **Threat Hunter** — Look for adversaries that existing detection missed, using hypotheses rather than alerts

- **Digital Forensics Examiner** — Produce forensically sound evidence that will survive legal, regulatory or disciplinary scrutiny

- **Detection Engineer** — Build and maintain the detection logic that turns telemetry into reliable signal
- **Security Engineer** — Build and run the security controls that the architecture calls for
- **Vulnerability Management Analyst** — Find, prioritise and drive the fixing of vulnerabilities before they are exploited
- **Cryptography and PKI Engineer** — Run the organisation's keys, certificates and cryptographic controls so that trust holds and nothing expires unexpectedly
- **Secrets and Configuration Management Engineer** — Give every credential and configuration value a managed lifecycle, so nothing important lives in a repository, a wiki or someone's terminal history

- **Security Awareness and Training Manager** — Change what people actually do, not what they can recite in a quiz
- **Insider Threat Analyst** — Detect and address harm caused by people with legitimate access, within strict legal and ethical limits
- **Purple Team Lead** — Measure and improve detection and response by running offensive techniques with the defenders in the room
