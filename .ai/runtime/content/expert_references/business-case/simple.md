# Investment business case — Field Appointment Reliability

<a id="dcs-business-case.investment_request"></a>
## Investment request and accountability

Request conditional authorisation to invest in **managed scheduling SaaS with a one-way dispatch sync** to address repeated double-bookings and delayed customer confirmations. Business sponsor: Field Operations Manager; delegated cost owner: Finance Business Partner (not yet named in the illustrative record). Funding envelope £140,000 over 2027–2029; no capital commitment is implied. Expected decision forum: Operations Director.

Target outcome is reduce appointment rework from an illustrative 2.3% to at most 0.5% within two quarters. Benefits ownership remains with the operating function rather than automatically transferring to the implementation team.

<a id="dcs-business-case.investment_options"></a>
## Investment choices and do-nothing baseline

Three options within the same illustrative three-year horizon: **no investment / baseline** incurs £80,000 of legacy operation or remediation cost; **preferred** managed scheduling SaaS with a one-way dispatch sync incurs £140,000 and could produce £240,000 of quantifiable benefits; **alternative** bespoke scheduler on the existing platform incurs £310,000 against £270,000 in modelled benefits.

The alternative may have different delivery risk, maintenance burden and portability. Baseline benefits are not double-counted in the preferred option. Finance must independently verify that benefits are genuinely incremental to the baseline and not already recognised elsewhere.

The decision is most sensitive to actual adoption, interface complexity and supplier charges. Trigger a reforecast if forecast TCO rises by more than 15%, if annual benefit realisation is below 80% of plan for two reporting periods or if 8 hours cannot be demonstrated. The financial model does not justify relaxing a security or statutory control.

Dependency chain: validated baseline → target architecture → phased implementation → operational readiness → benefits measurement. Risks: vendor availability and duplicated appointment updates; accountable risk owner: Head of Operations.

<a id="dcs-business-case.benefit_realisation"></a>
## Benefit-realisation plan and follow-up

Benefits owner: Field Operations Manager; financial tracking: Finance Business Partner; technical operating measures: weekly duplicate-booking rate and reconciliation exception count. The baseline must be collected from source systems for at least one representative operating cycle before go-live. Review at 30, 90 and 180 days after launch, then quarterly; record realised net benefits separately from avoided costs, non-cash service quality and compliance risk reduction.

If milestones are missed, suspend discretionary rollout and request an updated business case, not retrospective rewriting of original assumptions. The committee must decide whether to approve stage 1, conditionally defer or reject, and maintain an immutable decision record.
